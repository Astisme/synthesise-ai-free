# Market intelligence and research protocol

## Research question first

State the decision the research must inform. Define geography, customer segment, category, time horizon and exclusions.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer primary customer evidence (interviews, observed workflows, purchase behaviour); primary market evidence (competitor pages, official documentation, public filings, regulation); first-party customer language (reviews and public communities, cautiously interpreted); then reputable secondary research with transparent methods. Search snippets and unsourced summaries are discovery leads, not sole support for material claims.

## Competitor and alternative matrix

Capture provider and URL; direct/indirect/DIY/status quo; target segment and job; promise and mechanism; format and scope; public price, currency, billing basis and date; proof; distribution; strengths and limitations; unmet needs; confidence and source.

Do not assume public list price is the price every customer pays. Do not infer sales, market share, margins or satisfaction from marketing copy.

## Customer problem research

Look for repeated, specific descriptions of trigger/context, workarounds, switching costs, frequency/severity, time/money cost, desired outcome, failed solutions, buying process and budget owner. A convenience sample does not establish market prevalence.

## Synthesis

Deliver findings with citations and access dates, patterns and counterexamples, segment differences, meaningful positioning map, whitespace hypotheses, product implications, confidence, limitations and questions for primary research.

## Citation discipline

Cite material factual claims near the claim. Record source title, publisher, URL and publication/access date when available. Prefer recent sources for changing prices and features. Never fabricate a citation. If browsing is unavailable, state that the market picture is unverified and provide a research plan.
