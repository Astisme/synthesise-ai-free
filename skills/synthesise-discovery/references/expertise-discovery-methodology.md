# Expertise discovery methodology

## 1. Establish context and boundaries

Clarify the creator's objective, relevant career/life context, preferred outcomes, excluded topics, privacy boundaries and intended use of the profile. Do not solicit sensitive personal history unless clearly necessary and explicitly volunteered.

## 2. Map experience and capability

Inventory roles, responsibilities, industries, projects, specialist knowledge, tools, methods and recurring problem types. For each capability record depth, recency, frequency of use, context, independence, complexity and ability to explain or teach. Distinguish exposure, working competence and expert performance.

## 3. Extract tacit knowledge

Use concrete critical incidents and walkthroughs. Ask how the creator recognizes a problem, what signals they notice, what they do first, which decisions they make, why alternatives are rejected, what exceptions change the method and what mistakes novices make. Request examples, counterexamples and a teach-back. Convert intuitive actions into steps without pretending every judgment can be fully codified.

## 4. Examine achievements and evidence

For each claimed result capture starting condition, scope, creator contribution, collaborators, method, resources, timeframe, outcome, measurement and corroboration. Classify support as independently verified, documented, creator-reported, anecdotal or unsubstantiated. Do not attribute team outcomes solely to the creator or turn correlation into causation.

## 5. Identify intellectual assets

Inventory frameworks, checklists, templates, decision rules, teaching methods, datasets, terminology and combinations of disciplines. Compare each with common industry practice. Record origin, ownership/IP constraints, reuse permission, distinctiveness and evidence of practical utility. A renamed standard practice is not automatically proprietary.

## 6. Assess transferability and teachability

For each capability ask whether it is repeatable, explainable, teachable, adaptable to another context and likely to produce observable outcomes. Identify tacit dependencies, prerequisites and cases where expert judgment is essential. Separate what the creator can do from what they can reliably help another person do.

## 7. Evaluate product potential without ranking the market

Describe possible audience applications, customer problems, format compatibility, creator interest, capacity and delivery constraints. Do not rank customer demand or select the UVZ. Flag missing proof and the cheapest verification action.

## 8. Build the evidence register

Use stable evidence IDs. Record claim, evidence type, source/document, date, status, confidence, scope, sample, limitations and downstream use. Preserve the difference between creator-reported achievement and independent verification.

## 9. Required profile structure

Produce creator overview; expertise inventory; demonstrated capabilities; intellectual assets; achievement/evidence register; distinctive knowledge; possible audience applications; constraints/boundaries; unresolved questions; recommended next steps.

## 10. Quality review

Check for invented credentials, unsupported success claims, generic expertise presented as differentiation, missing context, attribution errors, privacy concerns and evidence promoted beyond its actual status.
