# UVZ advanced methodology

This reference deepens interpretation and evidence discipline around the canonical seven-dimension UVZ rubric. It does not replace `uvz-scoring.md`.

## 1. Establish a traceable opportunity portfolio

Create 3–7 materially distinct hypotheses. Give each a stable opportunity ID and express it as creator advantage × audience/job/problem × mechanism × transformation × commercial hypothesis. Reuse existing expertise and audience artifacts; record exact source paths and versions. Do not create near-duplicate ideas by changing only the audience label or delivery format.

## 2. Map expertise to a problem

For each candidate, map:
- the creator capability and its evidence;
- the customer situation and job;
- the causal mechanism by which the capability may help;
- the customer action or capability that changes;
- the output or observable transformation;
- missing capabilities, dependencies and delivery constraints.

A plausible thematic connection is not yet a credible solution mechanism. Explain the causal chain and mark unsupported links as hypotheses.

## 3. Analyze differentiation

Test differentiation against actual alternatives, not against an imagined generic competitor. Examine specialist experience, unusual combinations, proprietary-but-demonstrable methods, access to a narrow audience, implementation support, and a materially better path to the outcome. Separate defensible advantage from brand language, novelty and personal preference. A common method can still be differentiated by context or execution, but explain why customers would notice and value the difference.

## 4. Preserve and interpret the seven-dimension score

Use the exact seven dimensions, definitions, weights, 1–5 rating scale, aggregation formula, interpretation bands and sensitivity rules in `uvz-scoring.md`. Never add dimensions, redistribute weights, change thresholds or alter treatment of missing evidence in this reference. Do not calculate a score from absent inputs. Show the source and confidence behind every rating and retain the original rating if later evidence changes; record a new assessment/version instead of overwriting history.

## 5. Interpret scores without false precision

Treat the aggregate as a structured comparison aid, not a probability, market-size estimate or validation result. Explain which dimensions drive the ranking and whether the ranking is robust. A one-point rating difference is not automatically meaningful when evidence is weak. Show rating uncertainty and distinguish a score gap from a meaningful strategic advantage.

## 6. Opportunity sensitivity and decision robustness

Apply the existing canonical sensitivity procedure exactly. In addition, explain the decision implication in plain language: which uncertain input could reverse the order, what evidence would resolve it, and whether the recommendation remains acceptable under plausible alternatives. Do not invent alternate scoring weights or use sensitivity analysis to quietly change the model.

## 7. Portfolio and evidence interpretation

Compare the leading candidates on common evidence coverage, creator capacity, customer access, dependencies and reversibility. Identify a portfolio option only where it is genuinely useful; do not obscure the need for one next test. Reusable evidence keeps its original scope, sample, date, confidence and limitations when consumed by UVZ.

## 8. Required decision narrative

For each leading candidate provide: strongest supporting evidence; weakest critical assumption; differentiation mechanism; feasibility constraint; score drivers; sensitivity result; disconfirming evidence sought; cheapest discriminating test; and conditions that would trigger reassessment.

## 9. Quality review

Reject fabricated inputs, untraceable ratings, duplicate hypotheses, score-only recommendations and claims that the highest score proves demand. Confirm that the exact canonical scoring reference was used and that the seven weights remain unchanged.
