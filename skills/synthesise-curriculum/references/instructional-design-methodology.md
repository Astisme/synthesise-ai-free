# Instructional design methodology

## 1. Define learner performance

Describe the learner, context, prerequisites and final performance. Write objectives with observable verbs and conditions/standards where useful. Separate knowledge recall, procedural skill, judgment and integrated performance.

## 2. Apply backward design

Start with the approved product outcome, identify acceptable evidence of learner performance, then design practice and feedback, and only then select explanations and content. Map each objective to at least one assessment and each assessment to its objective. Flag product promises that cannot be assessed or taught within the agreed scope.

## 3. Map competencies and prerequisites

Break complex performance into component competencies. Identify prerequisite relationships and common misconceptions. Sequence from foundational concepts to guided performance, independent practice and transfer. Do not equate a topic list with a competency map.

## 4. Choose instructional methods by objective

Use concise explanation for concepts, worked examples for unfamiliar procedures, demonstration for practical tasks, retrieval practice for durable recall, deliberate practice for skill, case analysis for judgment, simulation for decisions and projects for integrated performance. Select format based on learner context and accessibility, not creator preference.

## 5. Specify each learning unit

For every module/lesson record: objective, prerequisite, estimated effort, key concept, example, activity, learner artifact, feedback, assessment criteria, completion standard, likely misconception and remediation. Keep instruction proportionate to the task and avoid unnecessary content.

## 6. Assessment validity

Use assessment methods that elicit the intended performance. Distinguish self-report and satisfaction from demonstrated competence. Define rubrics, scoring anchors, acceptable evidence, feedback timing and reassessment conditions. Do not claim mastery from attendance or completion alone.

## 7. Practice, feedback and remediation

Provide worked-to-independent progression, meaningful repetition, timely specific feedback and opportunities to correct errors. Design remediation for prerequisite gaps and common misconceptions. Include optional challenge paths where learners advance at different rates.

## 8. Workload and accessibility

Estimate learner effort by activity, not video duration alone. Check cognitive load, pacing, device/bandwidth assumptions, language, captions/transcripts, readable materials and alternative participation modes. Mark any accessibility requirement not yet met.

## 9. Production specifications

For each asset define purpose, learner, owner, format, source material, acceptance criteria, dependencies, version and maintenance trigger. Assets may include lessons, workbooks, worksheets, templates, checklists, examples, assessments and instructor notes.

## 10. Pilot and improve

Pilot with representative learners where possible. Observe task completion, errors, time, assessment results, support requests and learner feedback. Separate usability/satisfaction from learning gains. Record changes and retain prior blueprint versions.

## 11. Quality review

Check objective-assessment alignment, progression, practice coverage, feedback, remediation, workload, accessibility and product-promise alignment. Do not promise mastery without valid performance evidence.
