# Product transformation architecture

## 1. Define the customer and the job

Name the target segment, user/buyer/beneficiary roles, triggering situation, job, current workaround and constraints. Link these to audience and validation artifacts. If evidence is provisional, keep the product concept provisional.

## 2. Specify the transformation

Describe the starting state and desired ending state in observable terms. Map intermediate milestones, customer actions, required capabilities, outputs and completion evidence. Distinguish the outcome the product enables from outcomes outside its control. Define realistic time, prerequisites and external dependencies.

## 3. Engineer the transformation path

For each milestone specify:
- customer input and starting condition;
- decision or action required;
- method/mechanism and why it should work;
- tool, instruction or support needed;
- expected artifact or behaviour;
- acceptance criteria and common failure modes;
- recovery/remediation path.

Check sequence, bottlenecks, cognitive/operational load and dependencies. Avoid content modules without a customer action or outcome.

## 4. Evaluate the unique mechanism

Explain the causal logic connecting product activities to the outcome. Identify the principles, sequence, feedback, tools and implementation support. Distinguish a genuinely useful mechanism from a branded label. Compare with alternatives and identify what is materially different for the customer.

## 5. Select product format

Compare course, cohort, toolkit, playbook, workshop, membership, consulting, implementation service and software-enabled workflow. Evaluate outcome complexity, need for feedback, standardization, customer time, access, accessibility, support burden, maintenance, cost, creator capability and validation evidence. Choose the simplest format that can reliably deliver the promised transformation; do not choose software or recurring membership by default.

## 6. Bound scope and responsibilities

Define core components, optional elements, exclusions, prerequisites, customer responsibilities, provider responsibilities, support boundaries, delivery assumptions, privacy/security constraints and dependencies. Separate launch minimum from later roadmap items. Any promise expansion requires explicit owner/user approval and evidence review.

## 7. Design the customer journey

Map discovery, purchase, onboarding, orientation, implementation, progress checks, completion, support, renewal/continued use and exit/refund where applicable. Identify friction, handoffs, accessibility needs and failure recovery at each stage.

## 8. Define product quality and metrics

Set acceptance criteria for usability, completeness, reliability, outcome relevance, support response and operational feasibility. Select outcome and leading indicators with definitions, sources and review cadence. Avoid vanity measures as proxies for transformation.

## 9. Build roadmap and risk register

Prioritize by customer outcome, evidence, dependency, effort and reversibility. Record risks, assumptions, mitigations, owner and trigger. Distinguish must-have launch scope from optional enhancements.

## 10. Quality review

Verify the promise is evidence-bounded, every component advances the transformation, the format fits the job, exclusions and support are explicit, and the delivery system is feasible. Content volume is not product quality.
