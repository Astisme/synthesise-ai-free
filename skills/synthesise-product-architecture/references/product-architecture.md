# Product architecture

## Design from outcomes
Describe customer starting state, target state, constraints and observable completion evidence. Narrow the promise when results depend on customer effort or external factors.

## Choose a format
- Course: structured knowledge and practice.
- Cohort: guided implementation, feedback and accountability.
- Toolkit/templates: repeatable tasks where assets reduce friction.
- Workshop: bounded outcome in a short time.
- Playbook: repeatable process with decision points.
- Membership: ongoing value, updates or support.
- Productized service: customization or expert execution is essential.
- Software/AI workflow: recurring process can be reliably automated and maintained.

Choose based on customer job, complexity, support, access, cost and willingness to engage.

## Backward design for courses
For every outcome, state an observable action, define evidence, design practice and feedback, add only necessary instruction, and set completion/remediation criteria.

## Module specification
Each module includes name and purpose, outcome, prerequisites, concepts, activity sequence, practice and feedback, deliverable, completion criteria, estimated effort and contribution to transformation.

## Workbook and template design
A workbook includes instruction, prompt, worked example, workspace, quality checklist and next action. Templates include placeholders, usage guidance, constraints and a completed example.

## Quality gates
Every component advances an outcome; scope/prerequisites/exclusions are explicit; examples are realistic and labelled if fictional; activities produce artifacts; support and updates are feasible; accessibility, privacy, safety and legal constraints are considered; representative users test the product before broad release.
