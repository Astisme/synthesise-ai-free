# Market research methodology

## 1. Frame research around a decision

Write the decision question before searching. Specify segment, customer job, category boundaries, geography, time horizon, required evidence, exclusions and what finding would change the decision. Use focused research batches; broad market reports are not a substitute for decision-relevant evidence.

## 2. Build a market and alternative taxonomy

Classify direct competitors, adjacent solutions, substitutes, DIY approaches, internal workarounds and status quo. Define inclusion criteria and explain why each alternative belongs. Compare products on the same customer job and scope rather than their marketing category alone.

## 3. Establish source quality and triangulation

Prioritize primary sources for what a provider offers (pricing pages, product docs, terms, public filings) and direct customer evidence for experience. Use independent reporting and reputable datasets for market context. Triangulate material claims across independent source types where feasible. Record source conflicts; do not average incompatible definitions.

## 4. Build a comparable competitor record

For each alternative capture: provider, URL, audience, job, promise, mechanism, format, scope, delivery, support, prerequisites, price/currency/billing basis, observed date, proof claims, channel, strengths, weaknesses, review themes and unknowns. Mark unavailable data as unknown rather than infer it. Compare like-for-like scope and note bundle/discount limitations.

## 5. Analyze customer language carefully

Sample reviews, forums, interviews, communities, search queries and public questions using a stated collection method and date range. Code recurring pains, triggers, desired outcomes, objections, workarounds and unmet expectations. Preserve representative examples and counterexamples with links. Anecdotes are qualitative signals, not prevalence estimates; disclose platform, language, moderation and selection bias.

## 6. Identify gaps as hypotheses

A gap may involve an underserved segment, missing job, weak implementation support, complexity, cost, trust, accessibility or poor onboarding. For each gap state evidence, affected segment, alternatives examined, why the gap persists, potential solution implication and the cheapest validation step. Absence from a competitor page is not proof that a feature does not exist.

## 7. Pricing and market-size discipline

Record price as observed with currency, billing basis, included scope, discount/region/tax caveats and access date. Distinguish list price from realized price. Do not infer revenue, market share, conversion or customer count from pricing pages. For market sizing, define the population, geography, period, unit and source; label estimates and assumptions and show the calculation.

## 8. Research provenance

Every material claim links to source ID, title, publisher, URL/document locator, publication date if available, access date, excerpt/finding, claim status, confidence and limitation. Keep facts, provider claims, customer reports, interpretations and hypotheses separate. Re-check time-sensitive facts before publication.

## 9. Deliver decision-ready findings

Summarize the decision question, evidence strength, competitor/alternative matrix, customer language, price observations, gap hypotheses, positioning implications, counterevidence, research limitations and next tests. Cite claims near the finding; do not bury provenance in an untraceable appendix.

## 10. Quality review

Reject fabricated sources, unsupported market statistics, stale price claims presented as current, incomparable competitor matrices and unqualified market-gap claims. State explicitly when browsing or source access was unavailable.
