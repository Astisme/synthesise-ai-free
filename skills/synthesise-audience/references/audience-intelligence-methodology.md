# Audience intelligence methodology

## 1. Segment by situation and behaviour

Define candidate segments through triggering context, job, current behaviour, constraints, experience, alternatives and buying situation. Use demographics only when they materially affect access, needs or purchasing. Separate user, buyer, approver and beneficiary.

## 2. Jobs-to-be-done

For each segment capture the triggering event, functional progress, emotional progress, social progress, desired outcome, current workaround, competing priorities and switching forces. Phrase jobs in the customer's language where evidence exists. Avoid turning a product feature into a customer job.

## 3. Diagnose the problem

Investigate recent concrete episodes: what happened, frequency, context, consequence, urgency, workaround, cost and failed attempts. Separate observable symptoms from hypothesized causes. Note who experiences the problem and who bears the cost. Avoid treating intensity, frequency and willingness to pay as interchangeable.

## 4. Map transformation and constraints

Specify current state, desired state, observable success, time expectations, capabilities required, behavioural change, resources and obstacles. Identify dependencies outside the customer's control. Use measurable outcomes where possible and label aspirational outcomes as such.

## 5. Buying psychology

Identify trigger, decision process, budget owner, authority, procurement steps, perceived risk, trust requirements, alternatives, switching cost, objections and evidence of prior spend. Distinguish stated willingness from actual purchase behaviour. Do not infer budget from job title or problem severity.

## 6. Behavioural segmentation

Compare segments on problem context, urgency, existing workaround, sophistication, buying authority, adoption readiness and reachability. Explain meaningful within-segment variation. Avoid overfitting a persona from one vivid interview.

## 7. Audience accessibility

Map identifiable communities, professional networks, search intent, events, partnerships, owned audiences and relevant channels. Assess permission, cost, scale, credibility and access effort. Label untested channel ideas as hypotheses.

## 8. Evidence and interview practice

Record source, date, participant/segment scope, recruitment method, sample size, exact context and limitations. Ask about the last real occurrence and actual choices, not leading hypotheticals. Preserve negative and contradictory cases. Minimize personal data and obtain consent for recording or quotation.

## 9. Segment recommendation

Recommend a segment only after comparing fit, problem evidence, buying context and access. State why alternatives are weaker, what remains unknown and what evidence could change the recommendation. If evidence is insufficient, recommend further discovery rather than a false winner.

## 10. Quality review

Check segment distinctness, JTBD clarity, symptom/root-cause separation, user/buyer roles, evidence provenance, sample limitations, reachability and unsupported purchase assumptions.
