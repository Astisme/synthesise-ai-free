# Commercial and pricing methodology

## 1. Establish the commercial context

Confirm target buyer, buying situation, problem, desired outcome, approved product scope, alternatives, proof and delivery constraints. The offer must explain the value without expanding the product promise.

## 2. Analyze value without inventing monetary benefits

Examine time saved, cost avoided, risk reduced, capability gained, convenience and strategic value. Quantify only where a defensible baseline and calculation exist. State who receives value and who pays when they differ.

## 3. Research price and willingness to pay

Compare alternatives on scope, format, support, access, duration and terms. Record currency, billing basis, date and source. Separate observed list prices, customer-reported spend, actual paid commitments and proposed price hypotheses. Use interviews for context, not proof of a purchase; test actual commitment transparently where appropriate.

## 4. Architect the offer

Define core product, deliverables, onboarding, implementation support, feedback, access period, maintenance, prerequisites, customer responsibilities, exclusions and service levels. Add tiers only for distinct needs or support levels. Bonuses must remove a real obstacle or improve implementation, not inflate perceived volume.

## 5. Choose the revenue model

Compare one-time, installment, cohort, subscription, licensing and service-supported models. Assess fit with value timing, customer preference, cash flow, operational burden and retention. Recurring billing requires ongoing customer value and an explicit servicing obligation.

## 6. Model unit economics

Where inputs exist, calculate gross revenue, discounts, refunds, payment fees, variable delivery cost, support labor, fulfillment, acquisition cost, contribution and break-even volume. Define formulas and units. Separate fixed from variable costs and show excluded costs. Use scenarios for uncertain assumptions; never present projections as actuals.

## 7. Validate offer and price

Select one high-value uncertainty: tier comprehension, price acceptance, support demand, conversion or payment commitment. Predefine segment, protocol, denominator, thresholds and terms. Do not use deceptive scarcity, hidden fees or misleading guarantees.

## 8. Legal and ethical review

Flag refund, cancellation, tax, consumer-rights, privacy, licensing and advertising claims for jurisdiction-specific review. Do not provide unsupported legal conclusions. Make terms and limitations visible before purchase.

## 9. Required commercial strategy

Include positioning, value logic, offer structure, deliverables, tier rationale, price hypotheses, revenue model, unit economics assumptions, buyer objections, delivery obligations, validation tests and risks.

## 10. Quality review

Check scope alignment, evidence quality, currency/billing clarity, revenue-versus-profit distinction, omitted costs, recurring obligations and truthful claims.
