# Go-to-market methodology

## 1. Readiness gate

Verify product scope, delivery capacity, price/terms, proof permissions, audience access, checkout, onboarding, fulfillment, support, privacy, accessibility and escalation. Classify each as ready, blocked, at risk or not applicable. Do not recommend a public launch while critical fulfillment or customer-protection blockers remain hidden.

## 2. Set launch objectives

Define a decision-oriented objective, target segment, launch type (pilot, beta, cohort or general availability), time window, capacity and constraints. Establish baseline and success/failure thresholds before campaign execution.

## 3. Select channels deliberately

Compare audience concentration, trust, reach, cost, conversion path, creator capability, time to signal and sustainability. Select a small number of channels with explicit rationale. Distinguish owned, earned, partner and paid distribution. Do not recommend every channel by default.

## 4. Build messaging architecture

Connect audience situation, problem, desired transformation, mechanism, differentiation, proof, objections, offer scope and call to action. Match each claim to evidence and permission. Avoid guarantees, invented testimonials, inflated urgency and unsupported comparative claims.

## 5. Specify the conversion journey

Map source/channel → content or referral → landing/sales page → qualification → checkout/payment → confirmation → onboarding → delivery/support. Identify owner, system, friction, failure recovery and measurement at each step. Sales assets include page, demo, case evidence, FAQ, email, checkout and onboarding communications as relevant.

## 6. Plan campaign operations

Create a dated calendar with task, owner, dependency, effort, asset, channel, audience, acceptance criteria and status. Include pre-launch preparation, announcement, education, demonstration, objection handling, close/follow-up and customer onboarding. Make workload and budget assumptions explicit.

## 7. Define metrics

Use qualified reach, leads, conversion by stage, average order value, acquisition cost, refunds, activation, time-to-first-value, completion, support load and customer outcomes where relevant. Define numerator, denominator, source, cadence, owner and decision threshold. Attention metrics are not commercial outcomes.

## 8. Operational and customer safeguards

Test checkout, payment confirmation, refunds, accessibility, privacy, delivery access, support response, incident escalation and realistic fulfillment dates. Clearly label pilots/pre-sales and disclose what is not yet available.

## 9. Post-launch learning loop

Collect objections, lost-sale reasons, support issues, friction, refund reasons, usage, completion and outcome evidence. Segment findings by channel and customer type. Feed validated learnings to Audience, Product Architecture and Monetization through proposed changes; preserve artifact ownership and approvals.

## 10. Quality review

Check readiness blockers, claims/proof alignment, channel rationale, complete conversion path, task ownership, realistic budget/timing, metric definitions and a scheduled post-launch review.
