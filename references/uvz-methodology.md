# Unique Value Zone Methodology

## Purpose

Find the strongest intersection between a creator's expertise,
an audience's meaningful problems and a distinctive approach.

## 1. Expertise inventory

Identify:
- Professional experience.
- Practical skills.
- Specialized knowledge.
- Personal transformation.
- Repeatable results.
- Frameworks and processes.
- Unusual combinations of capabilities.

Distinguish demonstrated competence from aspiration.

## 2. Audience needs

For each potential audience, identify:
- Jobs they need to accomplish.
- Frustrations.
- Desired outcomes.
- Obstacles.
- Failed alternatives.
- Urgency.
- Buying context.

## 3. Opportunity intersection

For each opportunity answer:

1. What does the creator know or do unusually well?
2. Who needs that capability?
3. What costly or frustrating problem does it solve?
4. What is the specific transformation?
5. Why is this approach different?
6. Can the transformation be delivered consistently?

## 4. Comparative assessment

Assess opportunities qualitatively or on a 1–5 scale.

| Dimension | Assessment question |
|---|---|
| Expertise | Can the creator credibly deliver? |
| Need | Is the problem meaningful? |
| Urgency | Does the audience want a solution now? |
| Differentiation | Is the approach distinctive? |
| Accessibility | Can customers be reached? |
| Feasibility | Can the product be delivered? |
| Commercial potential | Is payment plausible? |

Scores are decision aids, not objective market measurements.

## 5. UVZ statement

Use this template:

"I help [specific audience] achieve [specific outcome]
by using [distinctive approach], drawing on my experience
in [relevant expertise]."

## 6. Output

Deliver:
- Three to five opportunity hypotheses.
- Comparative assessment.
- Recommended opportunity.
- UVZ statement.
- Supporting evidence.
- Unknowns and assumptions.
- Next validation experiment.
