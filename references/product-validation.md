# Product Opportunity Validation

## Objective

Determine whether an audience has a sufficiently meaningful
problem and whether the proposed solution deserves investment.

## Validation dimensions

1. Problem intensity.
2. Frequency.
3. Existing spending or effort.
4. Dissatisfaction with alternatives.
5. Urgency.
6. Audience accessibility.
7. Willingness to pay.
8. Product feasibility.

## Evidence hierarchy

Stronger evidence:
- Paid pilot purchases.
- Actual pre-orders.
- Repeated customer interviews with concrete examples.
- Existing spending on comparable solutions.
- Behavioural commitment.

Weaker evidence:
- Likes.
- Poll responses.
- Compliments.
- Hypothetical purchase intentions.
- Unverified market-size estimates.

## Customer interviews

Explore:
- Recent instances of the problem.
- Consequences.
- Existing workarounds.
- Costs in time and money.
- Previous attempts to solve it.
- What a successful outcome means.
- How buying decisions are made.

Avoid leading questions.

## Experiments

Choose the smallest experiment that reduces the most
important uncertainty.

Examples:
- Ten structured customer interviews.
- A landing page measuring qualified interest.
- A workshop or live pilot.
- A paid beta.
- A pre-sale with transparent delivery terms.

Define success criteria before running the experiment.

## Validation report

Include:
- Hypothesis.
- Method.
- Sample and limitations.
- Observations.
- Evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Decision.
- Next experiment.

Never label an idea validated solely because people say
they like it.
